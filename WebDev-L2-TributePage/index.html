HIS STORY / FOUR CHAPTERS

A curious mind that
never grew up

I1879 — 1900

The boy and the compass

Albert Einstein was born on 14 March 1879 in Ulm, Germany. As a child, he was captivated by a compass and the unseen forces that made its needle move. His fascination with mathematics and physics grew as he studied in Germany and Switzerland, eventually enrolling at the Zürich Polytechnic.

II1902 — 1905

A clerk with time to think

After graduating in Zürich, Einstein worked at the Swiss patent office in Bern. His spare hours were full of physics. In 1905, he published pioneering work on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion and special relativity, followed by his famous account of mass and energy.

III1915 — 1921

Bending the light of stars

Einstein completed the general theory of relativity in 1915. Four years later, astronomical observations during a solar eclipse supported his prediction that gravity bends light. The result made him internationally famous. He was awarded the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics, especially for explaining the photoelectric effect.

IV1933 — 1955

Genius with a conscience

After the rise of Nazism, Einstein settled in Princeton, New Jersey. Beyond physics, he spoke publicly for civil rights and peace and warned about the dangers of nuclear weapons. He loved music, especially the violin, and remained intellectually active until his death in 1955.

1879 — 1955

Milestones

Seventy-six years, a lifetime of discovery.

  1. 1879

    Born in Ulm

    Albert Einstein is born in southern Germany on 14 March.

  2. 1896

    Enters the Zürich Polytechnic

    He begins studying to teach mathematics and physics and graduates in 1900.

  3. 1905

    The miracle year

    His papers on light, atoms, special relativity and mass–energy equivalence change physics.

  4. 1915

    General relativity

    Einstein completes his theory of gravitation and curved spacetime.

  5. 1919

    Eclipse supports the theory

    Starlight measurements during a solar eclipse support a central prediction of general relativity.

  6. 1921

    Nobel Prize in Physics

    Awarded the prize for 1921, especially for the photoelectric effect.

  7. 1933

    A new home in Princeton

    He joins the Institute for Advanced Study in the United States.

  8. 1939

    Letter to President Roosevelt

    Einstein signs a letter warning that nuclear chain reactions could make powerful weapons possible.

  9. 1955

    Final days

    He dies on 18 April in Princeton, leaving a legacy that continues to shape science.
